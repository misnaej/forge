bump: minor
- **A pull-request wrap-up no longer spends its scarcest words telling you what it already told you.** The wrap-up renders two sections from the evidence — one for the local checks, one for continuous integration — and then asks its author for a summary and a recommendation. Measured across the newest wrap-up of twenty-four merged pull requests, half of them used those two slots to repeat the two sections: "suite green; pre-commit passes", directly above a section saying exactly that. Those lines held just over a quarter of every word the author contributed. The rule is now that those slots say what changed and what the reader has to decide, and the tool warns when one restates a section instead. It warns and still passes, like every other length and content rule here.
- **The warning matches a claim, never a mention.** A line that says the change *adds* a continuous-integration refresh, or *fixes* the local-check step, or that a reviewer flagged something now fixed, is describing work and stays silent — the distinction was checked against twenty-four real wrap-ups before the rule shipped, and found no false alarms in fifty-four lines of author prose. Saying that continuous integration *disagrees* with the local result is likewise untouched: reporting that is required, and nothing here compares the two values.
- **A pull-request description keeps release bookkeeping out of its summary.** No word counts for the repository's own documents, no note that a changelog entry exists, no check status outside the testing section. Thirty-seven per cent of merged descriptions carried one of those.
- **A commit message follows the same content rule as the squash message it will become**, which until now only the squash message stated.
